Shopify vs WooCommerce vs Custom Development: What’s Right for Your Business?

15 July 2026 · Published by Deepak Dupuguntla — you can reach the author at deepak@alpify.in

Picking the wrong e-commerce platform can cost you years of technical debt. We compare Shopify, WooCommerce and custom-built solutions across cost, scalability, speed and control — so you can make the right call for your stage and goals.

The decision that is hard to reverse

Platform migration is expensive, disruptive and risks your search rankings. Most businesses choose based on what a developer happened to recommend, then spend years working around limitations they did not know they were accepting.

The right choice depends on three things: how much you sell, how unusual your business logic is, and whether you have technical capacity in-house or on retainer.

The short answer: Shopify if you want to sell rather than manage infrastructure. WooCommerce if content and SEO drive your business and you need flexibility on a budget. Custom only if your business genuinely does something the platforms cannot express.

Shopify

A fully hosted platform. Shopify handles servers, security, payment compliance and updates. You handle products and marketing.

Where it wins

  • Speed to launch. A functioning store in days rather than weeks.
  • Reliability under load. Traffic spikes during sales are Shopify’s problem, not yours.
  • Security and compliance handled. PCI compliance, SSL and patching come included.
  • Strong app ecosystem. Most common requirements have a well-supported app.
  • Genuinely good checkout. Shopify’s checkout converts well and is continuously optimised.

Where it constrains you

  • Recurring cost that scales. Subscription plus transaction fees plus paid apps — app costs in particular accumulate quietly.
  • Limited checkout customisation below the highest tier.
  • Content management is secondary. If your strategy is content-led SEO, the blogging tools will frustrate you.
  • You do not own the platform. Pricing changes, policy changes and app deprecations are outside your control.

WooCommerce

An e-commerce layer on WordPress. Self-hosted, open source, and highly flexible — with the responsibilities that come with running your own infrastructure.

Where it wins

  • Content and SEO strength. WordPress remains excellent for content-led growth, which matters enormously in the Indian market where organic search drives a large share of discovery.
  • No transaction fees beyond what your payment gateway charges.
  • Full ownership. Your data, your server, your code — you can modify anything.
  • Lower cost at low volume. Hosting and a licence or two, rather than a growing subscription.
  • Strong local support availability. WordPress developers are abundant and affordable across India.

Where it costs you

  • You own maintenance. Updates, backups, security patches and uptime are your responsibility.
  • Performance requires work. A poorly configured WooCommerce store gets slow as the catalogue grows.
  • Plugin conflicts are real. Every plugin is a dependency that can break on update.
  • Security exposure. WordPress is heavily targeted; unmaintained sites do get compromised.

Custom development

Building the storefront and business logic specifically for your operation.

When it is genuinely justified

  • Unusual business logic — complex B2B pricing tiers, configurable products with dependencies, subscription models the platforms cannot express
  • Deep ERP or logistics integration where plugins introduce fragility
  • Very high transaction volume where platform fees exceed the cost of engineering
  • The technology itself is the product — marketplaces, multi-vendor platforms, anything where the software is the differentiator

What it demands

Significantly higher upfront investment, a longer timeline, ongoing engineering capacity, and the discipline to maintain what you built. Custom builds fail most often not at launch but eighteen months later, when the original developer has moved on and nobody understands the code.

Choosing honestly

  1. If you are starting out and want to validate demand — Shopify. Do not spend three months building before you know whether people will buy.
  2. If content and organic search are your growth engine — WooCommerce, provided you have someone maintaining it properly.
  3. If you have unusual logic the platforms genuinely cannot handle — custom, but be certain the limitation is real rather than something an app solves.
  4. If you are already selling well and hitting specific limits — identify the exact constraint before migrating. Many perceived platform limits are configuration problems.

The most expensive mistake: building custom too early. Businesses routinely spend heavily on bespoke platforms before validating that customers want the product. Validate on a platform, then build custom once you know precisely what you need — you will build the right thing.

What matters more than the platform

Page speed, mobile experience, checkout friction, product photography and clear delivery information affect conversion far more than the underlying platform. A well-executed WooCommerce store outperforms a poorly executed custom build every time. Choose a platform you can execute well with the resources you actually have — not the one that sounds most impressive.
