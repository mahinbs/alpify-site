GA4 vs Universal Analytics: Everything You Need to Know to Track Your Business in 2026

12 July 2026 · Published by Jatin Verma — you can reach him at jatin@alpify.in

Google Analytics 4 changed how we measure everything — from sessions to events to conversion paths. If you have not fully migrated and configured GA4 correctly, you are making decisions on incomplete data. Here is what is missing and how to fix it.

Why this still matters

Universal Analytics stopped processing data some time ago, yet a surprising number of Indian businesses are running GA4 in a default state — installed, collecting something, but never properly configured. The result is a dashboard that looks functional while producing numbers nobody should be making decisions on.

The fundamental change: sessions to events

Universal Analytics organised everything around sessions and pageviews. GA4 treats every interaction as an event — a pageview is an event, a click is an event, a purchase is an event, a scroll is an event.

This is not cosmetic. It is why your old reports do not exist, why familiar metrics moved or changed definition, and why numbers do not match what Universal Analytics reported. GA4 is not a redesign of the same tool; it measures differently.

Expect different numbers. GA4 typically reports fewer sessions and different bounce behaviour than Universal Analytics did for the same traffic. Neither was wrong — they count differently. Do not attempt to reconcile them; establish a new baseline and track trends from there.

The configuration most businesses have missed

1. Data retention is still at the default

GA4 defaults to the shorter retention window for exploration data. Almost nobody changes it, and then cannot run year-on-year comparisons. Change it to the maximum available in Admin → Data Settings → Data Retention. It is a two-click fix that cannot be applied retroactively — data already expired is gone permanently.

2. Key events are not configured

GA4 collects some events automatically, but it does not know which ones represent business value. Form submissions, phone clicks, WhatsApp clicks, quote requests, brochure downloads — these must be explicitly defined and marked as key events. Without this you can see traffic but not outcomes, which makes the whole installation close to decorative.

3. Internal traffic is not excluded

Your team browsing your own site inflates every metric. Configure internal traffic filters using your office IP addresses, and confirm the filter is set to active rather than testing — a step that is very commonly missed.

4. Google Ads and Search Console are not linked

Without these links you cannot see which campaigns or search queries drive conversions. Both connections take minutes and unlock reporting that is otherwise unavailable.

5. Cross-domain tracking is broken

If your checkout, booking system or payment gateway sits on a different domain, users appear as new visitors mid-journey and the original traffic source is lost — typically attributed to direct or referral. This makes paid campaigns look far less effective than they are.

6. Enhanced measurement is on but unverified

GA4 automatically tracks scrolls, outbound clicks, site search and file downloads. Useful — but the automatic site search tracking frequently fails to detect non-standard search parameters. Verify rather than assume.

Getting it right for Indian businesses specifically

  • Track WhatsApp and phone clicks as key events. A large share of Indian conversions happen off-site through a call or a WhatsApp message. If you only count form submissions, you are undercounting real leads substantially.
  • Import offline conversions. When a lead closes over the phone days later, feeding that back into GA4 and Google Ads is what makes attribution meaningful.
  • Segment mobile properly. Mobile behaviour differs sharply from desktop in India — analysing them together hides the pattern.
  • Watch tier-2 and tier-3 city performance. Growth increasingly comes from outside metros, and aggregate numbers conceal it.

The single highest-value fix: if you do nothing else, configure key events properly. Traffic numbers without conversion data cannot answer the only question that matters — which marketing activity produces customers.

A practical audit

  1. Is data retention set to the maximum?
  2. Are all meaningful actions defined and marked as key events?
  3. Is internal traffic excluded, with the filter active?
  4. Are Google Ads and Search Console linked?
  5. Is cross-domain tracking configured for every external domain in the journey?
  6. Are WhatsApp and phone clicks tracked?
  7. Does a test conversion appear correctly in real time?
  8. Is anyone actually reading the reports monthly?

Most businesses fail at least four of these. Each one is fixable in under an hour, and together they turn GA4 from a dashboard nobody trusts into a tool that answers real questions.

The point of all this

Analytics is not about collecting data — it is about making better decisions. If your GA4 setup cannot tell you which channel produces your cheapest customers, it is not configured well enough, however impressive the graphs look.
